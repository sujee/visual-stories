# History of Linux

## Goal
Trace Linux from its Unix roots to today: where it came from, how it spread, and how big it got. Published for Linux's 35th birthday (1991 → 2026).

## Audience
Anyone curious about tech. No prior knowledge needed.

## Story
- **Before Linux (1969–1990):** a clearly labeled prelude: Unix is born at Bell Labs, BSD, GNU and the free software movement, MINIX.
- **Scene change to 1991, titled "Just a hobby project":** introduced by "meanwhile, in Helsinki…" and the date (Aug 25, 1991), readable before the drop and kept on screen through the scene. The post stays up long enough to read in full. Then Linus Torvalds' announcement ("just a hobby, won't be big and professional"), on the music's drop. Linux 0.01 follows, then the first highlighted milestone, Linux 0.02's public announcement (see Must communicate). The timeline slows down through autumn 1991 so these moments can be read.
- **The Linux era (1991–2026):** distros branch out: Debian, Red Hat, Ubuntu, Arch, Android and more.
- Today: Linux runs the cloud, phones, every top supercomputer, even a helicopter on Mars.
- It ends by wishing Linux, with this exact text: "Happy 35th birthday, Linux." then "…and many, many more."

## Must communicate
- Linux is 35 (born 1991). The years before 1991 are its roots, labeled "Before Linux"; never call the whole span "57 years of Linux".
- A clear timeline of the major milestones.
- How the distros grew out of each other.
- Highlighted milestones, each emphasized as it happens (exact text):
  - OCT 5, 1991: Linux 0.02 is announced publicly
  - OCT 1994: Red Hat Linux debuts with its "Halloween" beta
  - AUG 1999: Red Hat IPO: one of the biggest first-day gains of the dot-com era
  - OCT 2004: Ubuntu 4.10: "Linux for human beings"
  - APR 2005: Linus writes Git in about 10 days
  - AUG 2006: Amazon EC2 launches: the cloud runs on Linux
  - SEP 2008: Android 1.0 ships: Linux heads for billions of phones
  - MAR 2013: Docker launches: containers go mainstream (built on Linux kernel features: cgroups and namespaces)
- The scale: from about 10,000 lines of kernel code to over 40 million.
